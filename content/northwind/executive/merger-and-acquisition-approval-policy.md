# Merger and Acquisition Approval Policy

## Purpose and Confidentiality

This policy governs how Northwind Systems evaluates and approves
mergers, acquisitions, and investments in other companies. It is a
private executive policy: it is visible only to the executive
department, and deal discussions are confidential under the IP and
Confidentiality Policy and the NDA Policy. The policy is owned by
the CEO, Dana Okafor, and the General Counsel, Amelia Brooks,
administers the deal process. This policy does not apply to
ordinary customer or vendor contracts, which follow the Contract
Review and Approval Policy. Every potential deal is registered in
the deal system with a code in the format M&A-#### before any
discussion with the counterparty.

## Approval Thresholds

Deals are approved by value. A deal with a total consideration below
$5 million is approved by the CEO alone, with the CFO informed.
A deal from $5 million to $25 million requires the approval of the
CEO, the CFO, and the General Counsel. A deal above $25 million
requires the board's approval, per the Board Reporting Calendar,
and the board must approve the deal before any binding commitment
is made. Total consideration includes cash, stock, assumed debt,
and earn-outs. The thresholds apply to the aggregate value of
related transactions: two deals with the same counterparty within
12 months are treated as one for approval purposes. Deals below
the thresholds but in a new market or a new product category are
reviewed by the board anyway, because of the strategic risk.

## Confidentiality and NDAs

No deal discussion starts without a signed NDA under the NDA
Policy, covering both sides. The NDA is signed before the first
meeting, and the deal code (M&A-####) is attached to the NDA. Deal
information is shared on a need-to-know basis: the deal team is
named by the CEO, and the team is limited to the executives, legal,
and the finance staff assigned to diligence. Deal information is
not discussed with employees outside the deal team, and the
standard rule is that the target company's name is not shared
inside Northwind until the deal is announced or the process ends.
A confidentiality breach during a deal is handled by the General
Counsel and is grounds for termination under the Termination and
Offboarding Policy.

## Due Diligence

Due diligence runs for at least 30 days for every deal, and the
diligence plan is approved by the General Counsel before it
starts. The diligence covers: financials (led by the CFO's team),
legal and contracts (led by legal), security and data (led by the
CISO's team, using the Security and Vulnerability Policy's
standards), HR and employment (led by the CHRO), and customer and
market (led by the COO). Diligence findings are consolidated in a
diligence report that the CEO reviews before any approval is
sought. A material finding (unreported liabilities, a security
breach, a customer concentration risk) is reported to the board
even for deals below the board threshold. Diligence materials are
retained for ten years.

## Exclusivity and Process Rules

Exclusivity agreements (the target agrees not to talk to other
buyers) are limited to 60 days, renewable once with the CEO's
approval. Letters of intent are approved at the level of the deal
threshold (the same approval chain as the final deal), because a
letter of intent commits Northwind to the process. No public
statement about a deal is made without the CEO's approval, and the
announcement plan is part of the board approval package for deals
above $25 million. Deals that involve the acquisition of a company
with employees trigger the HR integration plan, which the CHRO
prepares before signing, covering the Termination and Offboarding
Policy's application to the acquired staff.

## Integration and Post-Deal

After a deal closes, the integration plan is owned by the COO, with
the deal code (M&A-####) carried into the integration project. The
integration plan covers: systems and data migration (under the
Change Management Policy and the System Log Retention Policy),
customer communication (under the Customer Onboarding Checklist's
communication standards), and employee onboarding (under the
Recruiting and Interview Policy's handover). The acquired
company's contracts are reviewed under the Contract Review and
Approval Policy, and its vendors under the Vendor and Procurement
Policy. Integration milestones are reported to the board at the
next two board meetings, and the deal is reviewed for value
realization at the 12-month mark.

## Relationship to Other Policies

The Contract Review and Approval Policy covers ordinary contracts;
deal documents (the purchase agreement, the exclusivity agreement)
are governed by this policy instead. The NDA Policy covers the
confidentiality agreements that start every deal. The Executive
Severance Policy's change of control provisions apply to
executives affected by a deal, and they are approved by the board
as part of the transaction. The Dispute Resolution and Arbitration
Policy governs post-deal disputes with the seller. The Board
Reporting Calendar covers the board approvals and the integration
reporting. The Data Retention Policy covers the retention of deal
records (ten years for board and deal records).
