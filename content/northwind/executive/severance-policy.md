# Executive Severance Policy

## Purpose and Confidentiality

This policy defines severance terms for Northwind Systems executive
officers: the CEO, the C-suite (CFO, CTO, CHRO, CISO, COO, General
Counsel), and vice presidents. It is a private board document: it is
visible only to the executive department, and its contents are not
shared with employees, managers, or the HR department except where
HR administers a departure under the Termination and Offboarding
Policy. The policy is approved by the board of directors and is
reviewed annually at the December board meeting (see the Board
Reporting Calendar). The standard employee termination terms are in
the Termination and Offboarding Policy; this policy covers only the
executive levels named here.

## CEO Severance

The CEO's severance is set by the board. If the CEO is terminated
without cause (or resigns for good reason, as defined in the CEO
employment agreement), the CEO receives: 24 months of base salary,
paid in normal payroll installments; 100% acceleration of unvested
equity, with all unvested options and restricted stock vesting on
the termination date; and continuation of health benefits for 24
months at the active employee rate. The CEO's severance requires
board approval and is not within the CEO's own authority. If the
CEO is terminated for cause, no severance is paid, and equity
unvested at termination is forfeited. The CEO's severance is not
subject to the non-solicit waiver process; the 12-month non-solicit
clause in the IP and Confidentiality Policy applies in all cases.

## C-Suite Severance

C-suite officers (CFO, CTO, CHRO, CISO, COO, General Counsel)
terminated without cause receive: 12 months of base salary, paid in
normal installments; 50% acceleration of unvested equity; and 12
months of health benefit continuation at the active employee rate.
C-suite severance is approved by the board, on the recommendation
of the CEO; the CEO cannot approve a C-suite severance alone. A
C-suite officer terminated for cause receives no severance. A
C-suite officer who resigns voluntarily receives no severance,
except in a good-reason resignation as defined in their employment
agreement (material reduction in role, relocation without consent,
or a breach of the agreement by the company). Good-reason
resignations are reviewed by the board.

## VP Severance

Vice presidents terminated without cause receive: 6 months of base
salary, paid in normal installments; no equity acceleration
(unvested equity is forfeited, and vested equity is exercisable for
90 days per the Termination and Offboarding Policy); and 6 months
of health benefit continuation. VP severance is approved by the CEO
with the CHRO's review; the board is not involved except for the
annual review of the policy. A VP terminated for cause receives no
severance. VP severance does not include bonus or commission
payouts beyond what is earned under the Sales Commission Plan, and
the commission plan's clawback rules apply to any commission paid
in the 90 days before termination.

## Change of Control

In a change of control (a merger or acquisition covered by the
Merger and Acquisition Approval Policy), the severance terms above
are replaced by the change of control provisions: executives who
are terminated without cause within 12 months of the change of
control receive their severance under this policy, doubled: the CEO
receives 24 months (unchanged, as the base already reflects the
change of control), and C-suite officers receive 24 months with
100% equity acceleration. The change of control provisions apply
only if the executive's role is eliminated or materially reduced;
an executive who stays in an equivalent role is not eligible. The
board approves the change of control provisions as part of the
transaction approval under the Merger and Acquisition Approval
Policy.

## Board Approval and Administration

All executive severance agreements require board approval, including
the CEO's own agreement (approved by the board without the CEO's
vote). The board reviews the severance policy annually at the
December board meeting, and any change to the policy takes effect
on January 1 of the following year. HR administers the payments
under the Termination and Offboarding Policy's final pay rules (72
hours for earned pay), but HR does not set the terms and does not
negotiate them. Severance agreements are contracts under the
Contract Review and Approval Policy's records rules: they are
retained for seven years after the agreement ends, per the Data
Retention Policy. The board's compensation committee reviews the
severance terms at its June meeting (see the Board Reporting
Calendar).

## Confidentiality of This Policy

This policy is private. Its existence and its terms are not
published on the intranet, are not in the Employee Handbook, and
are not described in any employee-facing document. The standard
termination documents (the Termination and Offboarding Policy)
refer to executive terms only as "terms per the executive
agreement." Directors and executives who share this policy outside
the executive department breach the IP and Confidentiality Policy,
and the breach is handled by the board. Questions about this
policy go to the General Counsel, Amelia Brooks, who is the
custodian of the executive agreements.
