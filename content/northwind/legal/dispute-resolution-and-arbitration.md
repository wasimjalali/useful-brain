# Dispute Resolution and Arbitration Policy

## Scope

This policy governs how Northwind Systems resolves disputes with
customers, vendors, partners, and former employees. It is owned by
legal and is read by the sales team, who are usually the first to hear
about a dispute. The standard customer contract incorporates this
policy's process, so the dispute clauses are already in the MSA (see
the Contract Review and Approval Policy). Day-to-day customer
frustration is not a dispute: it is handled through the Customer
Complaint Escalation Path, which runs in parallel and has its own
levels (ESC-1 through ESC-4). A dispute starts when a party claims a
breach, an unpaid invoice, or a liability, and the claim is not
resolved through the complaint path.

## Negotiation Period

Before any formal step, both sides negotiate for 60 days. The clock
starts when either side sends a written dispute notice; Northwind's
dispute notices are sent by legal with a code in the format DSP-####.
During the 60 days, the parties exchange information and meet at least
once, in person or by video. The sales account owner participates in
negotiations for customer disputes, and the vendor manager participates
for vendor disputes under the Vendor and Procurement Policy. Settlement
authority during negotiation: the finance manager can settle disputes
up to $5,000, the CFO up to $25,000, and anything above $25,000 needs
the CEO's approval. Settlements are documented as amendments to the
underlying contract.

## Mediation

If the 60-day negotiation does not resolve the dispute, either party
can suggest mediation. Mediation is voluntary and non-binding, and it
is held in Austin unless both parties agree otherwise. A single
mediator is selected by agreement within 15 business days, and the
mediation is scheduled within 60 days of selection. Mediation costs are
split equally unless the parties agree otherwise. Northwind's
settlement authority during mediation is the same as during
negotiation. If mediation is declined or fails, the dispute moves to
arbitration. Mediation is not required before arbitration; it is an
option either party can propose.

## Arbitration

Disputes that survive negotiation go to binding arbitration. The
arbitration is administered by a neutral arbitration service, seated in
Austin, Texas, with a single arbitrator, and the arbitration clause is
in the standard MSA. The arbitrator is selected within 20 business days
of the arbitration demand. The arbitration follows the service's
rules, the hearings are confidential, and the arbitrator's award is
final and binding, with limited grounds for appeal. Claims under
$10,000 are handled at the customer's option through small claims
court instead of arbitration, at the customer's choice of venue.
Arbitration is conducted in English, and each side bears its own costs
unless the arbitrator awards costs to the prevailing party.

## Special Dispute Types

Billing disputes follow the Invoicing and Payment Terms Policy first:
a disputed invoice is not late while the dispute is open, and billing
disputes unresolved after 30 days move to this policy. Refund disputes
follow the Refund Policy's appeal step first, then the complaint path,
then this policy; the 14-day refund window does not restart because a
dispute is filed. Employment disputes (including former employees)
are handled by HR and legal together, and the standard employment
agreement incorporates the same arbitration process for claims above
the small claims threshold. Vendor disputes follow this policy through
the Vendor and Procurement Policy's vendor manager. IP disputes are
excluded from arbitration and are litigated in the courts specified in
the IP and Confidentiality Policy.

## Litigation Holds

When a dispute reaches the arbitration stage, or when litigation is
threatened, legal issues a litigation hold (HLD-#### codes) covering
the records related to the dispute. The hold suspends deletion under
the Data Retention Policy for the affected records until legal lifts
the hold. The hold is issued to the departments involved within five
business days of the arbitration demand, and legal confirms receipt.
Destruction of records under an active hold is a violation of the Data
Retention Policy and is handled as misconduct. Legal reviews open
holds quarterly and lifts them when the dispute closes.

## Costs and Escalation

Dispute costs (arbitration fees, external counsel) are tracked by
legal against the dispute code and reported to the CFO quarterly.
Disputes where the amount claimed exceeds $100,000 are reported to the
CEO when the arbitration demand arrives, and the board is informed at
the next board meeting per the Board Reporting Calendar. The General
Counsel, Amelia Brooks, approves all settlement documents before
signature, regardless of amount. Dispute outcomes are logged in the
legal system and reviewed annually for patterns (for example,
recurring contract clauses that generate disputes), and the annual
review feeds the contract template updates in the Contract Review and
Approval Policy.
