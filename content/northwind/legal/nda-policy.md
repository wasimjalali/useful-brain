# NDA Policy

## Purpose

This policy governs nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) between Northwind
Systems and outside parties. It covers when an NDA is needed, who signs
it, and which template to use. The policy is owned by legal and applies
to everyone who shares Northwind information with third parties:
primarily sales, but also engineering (for integration discussions),
finance (for vendor negotiations), and the executive team (for
strategic discussions). The IP and Confidentiality Policy covers
internal confidentiality; this policy covers the agreements that protect
confidentiality externally. Questions go to legal@northwind.example.

## When an NDA Is Required

An NDA must be signed before any confidential information is shared
with a third party. This includes: customer evaluations and proof of
concept discussions, vendor and partner negotiations, integration
partner technical discussions, and any discussion of pricing, roadmap,
or financial data. Public information (marketing materials, published
pricing, press releases) does not need an NDA. A customer who wants
Northwind to sign their NDA instead of ours is handled as a deviation
(see below). Engineering shares API documentation only after an NDA is
in place for pre-release versions; released documentation is public and
needs no NDA. Sales uses the evaluation NDA template for prospects who
want pricing before a contract.

## Templates and Terms

Northwind has three NDA templates: the mutual NDA (standard for
partnerships and integrations), the one-way NDA (Northwind discloses,
used for customer evaluations), and the vendor NDA (used in
procurement). All templates share the same core terms: a two-year
term, confidentiality obligations covering written and oral
information, an exception for information that is public through no
fault of the receiving party, and no license grant for IP. The standard
term is two years from signing; a shorter or longer term is a deviation.
Templates are stored in the contract system and are signed
electronically with codes in the format NDA-####.

## Signing Authority

NDA signing authority: any member of the legal team can sign any NDA.
Sales directors can sign the one-way evaluation NDA without legal
review. The mutual NDA requires legal review before signature if the
counterparty redlines it; if the counterparty signs our template
unredlined, the sales director can sign it. The vendor NDA is signed
by the operations director under the Vendor and Procurement Policy.
Redlined NDAs, or any request to sign a counterparty's NDA form, go to
legal, which reviews within three business days. The executive team
signs NDAs for strategic discussions using the mutual template with
legal review, per the Merger and Acquisition Approval Policy's
confidentiality requirements.

## Handling Counterparty NDAs

When a counterparty insists on their own NDA form, legal reviews it
against our minimum terms: term no longer than three years, no
unilateral audit rights, no indemnification of the counterparty's
employees, and no restrictions on our ability to do business with
others. If the counterparty NDA meets the minimum terms, legal approves
it with a memo; if not, legal proposes our template or a redline. The
review takes up to five business days. NDAs signed on a counterparty's
form are still registered in the contract system with an NDA-#### code
and are subject to the same retention as contract records (seven years
after the agreement ends, per the Data Retention Policy).

## NDA Administration

All NDAs are registered in the contract system before confidential
information is shared; an unregistered NDA is treated as no NDA. Legal
maintains the NDA register, which records the counterparty, the
template, the signatories, and the expiry date. The register is
reviewed monthly by legal, and NDAs expiring within 90 days are flagged
for renewal or closure. Sharing confidential information under an
expired NDA is a breach of the IP and Confidentiality Policy. NDAs are
company records: they are not filed with the department that signed
them, they live in the contract system, and access is limited to legal,
finance, and the signatory's department head.

## Relationship to Other Policies

The Contract Review and Approval Policy governs the contracts that
follow NDAs; an NDA is not a contract for that policy's thresholds and
does not require CFO approval at any value. The Dispute Resolution and
Arbitration Policy applies to NDA disputes like any other contract
dispute, and the standard NDA incorporates the same 60-day negotiation
and binding arbitration clauses. The Merger and Acquisition Approval
Policy requires NDAs for all diligence conversations about potential
deals, signed before the first discussion. The Regulatory Compliance
Calendar includes the monthly NDA register review as a legal control.
