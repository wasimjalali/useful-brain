# Training and Development Budget

## Purpose

Every regular employee at Northwind Systems receives an annual training
and development budget of $2,500. The budget exists to support skills that
connect to the employee's current role or a documented career path; it is
not a personal perk. This document explains how the budget is allocated,
approved, and spent, and is administered by HR. Because it contains budget
figures, it is shared only within HR. Employees learn the amount and the
rules from their manager during onboarding and from the HR portal; the
portal shows each employee their remaining balance for the year.

## Annual Allocation and Rollover

The $2,500 allocation renews on January 1 each year. Unused budget does
not roll over: spending must be committed (course booked, conference
registered, or materials ordered) by December 31. The only exception is
for employees on parental leave: their unused balance carries into the
next year once, and only if they were on leave for more than eight weeks
in the prior year. The budget applies to full-time and part-time
employees pro rata (0.5 FTE receives $1,250). Interns and contractors are
not eligible. Total company training spend is capped in the annual
budget at 2% of payroll, set during the Budget Planning and Forecasting
cycle.

## What Is Covered

Covered expenses include: courses and certifications, conference tickets
(registration only, not travel; travel follows the Travel Expense
Policy), books and subscriptions related to the role, exam fees, and
coaching. The budget can be used for a mix of these, and a single
certification costing more than the annual budget can be split across two
years with HR approval, provided the first year's portion is committed
before December 31. Not covered: equipment (laptops, monitors, headsets),
tuition for degree programs, personal development unrelated to the role,
and membership fees not tied to a professional body relevant to the job.
If an expense is borderline, the HR manager decides.

## Approval Process

The employee submits a training request in the HR portal with the course,
the cost, and a one-line justification tied to their role or career path.
Requests up to $1,000 are approved by the employee's manager. Requests
above $1,000 need the department head as well. HR checks the request
against the budget and the covered list, and the approval decision is
communicated within five business days. A request can be denied if the
training conflicts with a mandatory compliance training window in the
Regulatory Compliance Calendar, or if the department is in a hiring
freeze (training is paused during freezes, which HR announces by email).
Denials include the reason, and the employee can appeal to the CHRO.

## Reimbursement and Payment

For courses the employee pays for out of pocket, reimbursement follows
the Employee Expense Policy: receipts are required at $25 and above, and
reimbursement is paid within 10 business days of approval. For courses
Northwind pays directly, the employee books through the HR portal and
invoices go to finance with the approval code (TR-####). If the employee
leaves Northwind within 12 months of a training expense above $1,000,
they repay the amount pro rata, deducted from final pay under the
Termination and Offboarding Policy; this obligation is signed at approval
time. Certifications funded by the budget are logged in the HR system and
appear on the employee's internal profile.

## Manager Guidance

Managers should discuss development at the quarterly check-in, not only
at the annual review. The March review cycle is when most training plans
are set, and the review system includes a development plan field. Managers
can request team training (for example, a workshop for the whole team)
from the same budget, pooled across the team, with the same approval
chain; a pooled request over $2,500 needs the department head. HR reports
budget usage by department each quarter to the CHRO, and departments
that consistently underuse (below 40% of their pooled allocation) are
asked for an explanation at the annual budget review.

## Record Keeping

Training records, including certificates and receipts, are kept by HR for
six years after employment ends, consistent with the Data Retention
Policy's HR records rule. Budget usage data feeds the annual training
report to the CHRO and CFO in January, which informs the next year's 2%
of payroll allocation. Audit access to training spend is reviewed by the
CHRO each quarter.
