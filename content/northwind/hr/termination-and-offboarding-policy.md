# Termination and Offboarding Policy

## Purpose and Audience

This policy describes how employment ends at Northwind Systems, for both
voluntary resignations and involuntary terminations, and what happens in
the offboarding process. It is written for managers and the HR team;
employees learn the practical details from HR at the time of their exit.
The policy is owned by HR and administered by the HR manager for each
office. Questions go to hr@northwind.example. Offboarding touches several
other policies: equipment return is covered by the Equipment Return
Policy, and final access revocation is coordinated with IT per the
Security and Vulnerability Policy.

## Voluntary Resignation

Employees resign by submitting notice in the HR portal. The standard
notice period is four weeks; the minimum acceptable is two weeks, and
anything shorter must be approved by the department head. Managers accept
the resignation, schedule a handover, and notify HR the same day. During
the notice period the employee keeps normal access and duties; garden
leave (paid time away from work during notice) is used only when the
manager and HR agree there is a risk to customer relationships or
confidential information. Counteroffers are permitted but rare: the
recruiting policy's offer approval thresholds apply to retention offers
above $150,000, meaning a director must approve them.

## Involuntary Termination

Involuntary terminations follow a strict approval chain. A manager cannot
terminate alone. For employees up to L4, the manager proposes the
termination to the HR manager, and the department head approves. For L5
and above, the department VP must approve, and HR consults the CHRO.
Termination for cause (fraud, theft, gross misconduct, repeated policy
violations) follows the same chain but can move faster: the employee can
be suspended with pay for up to five business days while HR investigates,
and the investigation must conclude within ten business days. There is no
termination without a documented prior warning, except for cause cases.
Performance-based terminations must come out of a completed Performance
Improvement Plan under the Performance Review Policy.

## Final Pay

Final pay is processed within 72 hours of the employee's last working day,
for both voluntary and involuntary exits. Final pay includes: salary
through the last day, any accrued but unused statutory leave that local
law requires to be paid out, and any commissions or bonuses already earned
under the Sales Commission Plan rules. Deductions are limited to: amounts
the employee owes under a signed agreement (for example, equipment damage
over $500 per the Equipment Return Policy), and any overpayment of salary
in the prior 90 days. Deductions are itemized on the final pay statement.
The final paycheck is paid by the normal payroll run if it falls within
the 72-hour window, otherwise by a special off-cycle run.

## Benefits and Continuation

Health coverage continues through the end of the month in which
employment ends. After that, departing employees can buy Northwind's
health continuation coverage for up to 12 months at the full premium
plus a 2% administration fee, mirroring what local law provides where
applicable. HR sends the continuation enrollment form with the exit
paperwork; the form must be returned within 30 days of the last day.
Unvested equity is forfeited, and vested equity is exercisable for 90
days after the last day, after which unexercised options lapse. The
12-month non-solicit clause in the IP and Confidentiality Policy applies
to departing employees at manager level and above.

## Equipment and Access

All company equipment must be returned within 30 calendar days of the
last working day, per the Equipment Return Policy: laptops, phones,
badges, and any loaned peripherals. IT revokes system access at the end
of the last working day, except that departing employees keep email
access in read-only mode for five business days to forward personal
mail, unless the exit is for cause, in which case access ends
immediately. The manager is responsible for collecting the equipment
handover checklist and confirming data handover of team documents.
Failure to return equipment results in the cost of the equipment being
deducted from final pay, up to the item's book value.

## Exit Interview

HR conducts an exit interview for every departure, within 14 days of the
last working day. Exit interviews are confidential and are used
aggregately; individual feedback is not shared with the manager unless
the departing employee consents. Exit interview notes are kept by HR for
two years. Findings that indicate a systemic issue (for example, three or
more departures from the same team within six months) are escalated to
the CHRO and the department head. The final step of offboarding is the
HR manager closing the employee's record in the HR system, which
triggers the six-year HR record retention clock in the Data Retention
Policy.
