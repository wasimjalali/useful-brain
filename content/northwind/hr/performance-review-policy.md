# Performance Review Policy

## Purpose and Audience

This policy governs the annual performance review cycle at Northwind
Systems. It is written for people who run reviews: managers, directors,
and the HR team. Employees do not receive this document directly; they
receive their own review summary through the HR portal. The cycle produces
three outcomes: a rating, a compensation decision, and (where applicable)
a promotion. All three follow the timeline in this policy. The policy is
owned by HR, and questions go to the CHRO, Lena Fischer. Review records are
kept by HR for six years after employment ends, in line with the Data
Retention Policy.

## Review Cycle Timeline

The annual cycle runs on a fixed calendar. Reviews take place March 1
through March 31, when every manager holds a one-on-one review meeting
with each direct report. Calibration sessions run April 1 through April
15, where managers compare ratings across teams. Decisions are finalized
and communicated to employees by April 30. Compensation changes and any
promotions approved in the cycle take effect May 1. There is no mid-year
formal review, but managers are expected to hold quarterly check-ins that
are informal and not recorded in the review system. New hires who joined
after October 1 are reviewed on a shortened cycle in their first year and
receive a rating based on the period from their start date.

## Ratings

Every employee receives a single rating on a five-point scale: 5
(exceptional, top 5% of the company), 4 (exceeds expectations), 3 (meets
expectations, the standard for solid performance), 2 (development needed),
and 1 (unacceptable). Ratings are relative to the employee's level, not to
their tenure. A rating of 1 triggers a Performance Improvement Plan (see
below). Ratings of 4 and 5 are capped: no team may have more than 25% of
its members at 5, and the company-wide target is 5% at 5, 20% at 4, 60% at
3, 10% at 2, and 5% at 1, before calibration adjusts for team size. The
rating determines the compensation outcome: 4s and 5s are eligible for
merit increases, 3s for a standard cost-of-living adjustment, and 2s
receive no increase and a written development plan.

## Calibration

Calibration is mandatory for every team. Each department head chairs a
calibration session between April 1 and April 15 where managers present
their ratings, and the group challenges outliers. The CHRO attends the
sessions of every department at least once. Calibration exists to make
ratings comparable across teams: two employees doing the same job at the
same level should land at the same rating for the same output, regardless
of which manager reviewed them. After calibration, ratings are locked and
cannot be changed except by the department head with HR approval. Managers
who consistently produce ratings far from the company distribution (more
than two points of deviation in their team's average) are themselves
flagged in the calibration notes.

## Promotions

Promotions are decided in the same cycle and are effective the first day
of the quarter after approval, not on May 1. Approval authority by level:
L1 to L4 promotions need the director's approval, L5 and L6 need the
department VP, and L7 and L8 need the CEO or CTO. A promotion moves the
employee into the salary band of the new level, per the Salary Bands
document, and pay is set within the new band. Employees are eligible for
promotion after at least 12 months at their current level, except for
exceptional cases approved by the department VP. A promotion is not a
rating outcome: a 3-rated employee can be promoted if their manager makes
the case in calibration.

## Performance Improvement Plans

A rating of 1, or a rating of 2 in two consecutive cycles, triggers a
Performance Improvement Plan (PIP). A PIP lasts 60 days and includes
specific, measurable goals agreed between the employee, the manager, and
HR. The manager reviews progress at day 30 and day 60. Successful
completion returns the employee to a normal rating cycle. Failure to meet
the PIP goals leads to termination under the Termination and Offboarding
Policy, and that decision requires the department head's approval. PIPs
are confidential: only the employee, their manager, the department head,
and HR see them.

## Compensation Link

The review cycle feeds the compensation review, which HR runs in April
with finance. Merit increases and bonus payouts for the prior year are
communicated by April 30 and paid with the first paycheck in May. Band
changes from the March band review are also effective May 1, so a band
change and a merit increase can land in the same paycheck. Employees who
are below their band minimum after any change are flagged for a mandatory
catch-up review within six months, per the Salary Bands document. Budget
for merit increases is set in the Budget Planning and Forecasting cycle in
November of the prior year, at 3% of payroll by default.
