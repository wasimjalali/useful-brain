# Procurement Forms and Workflow Guide

## Which Form to Use

Procurement at Northwind follows a structured decision tree
based on the type, scope, and urgency of your purchase.
Selecting the right form at the start prevents delays,
returns, and compliance violations downstream. The form you
choose depends on whether you are purchasing something new,
renewing an existing contract, modifying an existing
purchase, responding to an unexpected need, or acquiring a
service rather than goods. This section walks you through
each common scenario and explains which form applies, who
reviews it, and what timeline to expect. Understanding these
categories prevents confusion and ensures your purchase
moves through the system without unnecessary rejections.

For a new purchase of goods or standard services, start
with a purchase requisition. This is the most common path
and applies whether you are buying office supplies,
equipment, software licenses, or contracted services like
consulting or maintenance. The requisition form captures
what you need, why you need it, who will approve it based
on cost, and attaches supporting quotes to prove you have
shopped for value. A requisition for a new hire's laptop,
a renewal of software subscriptions used across the
company, or a one-time consulting engagement for a specific
project all start with the same form. A renewal of an
existing vendor contract or subscription follows the same
process: raise a requisition against the renewal quote and
attach evidence that the pricing was competitive or
approved in the original contract terms. If you have been
paying an annual fee for a software license at the same
price for two years and the vendor is now offering a
renewal at a 10 percent increase, note that in the
requisition so the approver understands the price history
and the reason for the increase. If an existing purchase
order needs to change, such as adding scope, extending the
delivery date, changing quantities, or changing the
recipient department, initiate a change order rather than
canceling and restarting. A change order is faster,
preserves the audit trail, avoids the need to re-quote in
most cases, and maintains the original vendor
relationship. Using a change order instead of requisitioning
a new purchase also avoids creating duplicate vendor setup
work and multiple invoices for related items.

When time does not permit the normal process, an emergency
purchase can bypass the requisition stage entirely.
Emergency procurement is for situations where the need was
genuinely unforeseen and delay would cause operational harm,
financial penalty, or compliance violation. A critical
server in the data center fails unexpectedly and requires
an immediate replacement part to resume operations and
prevent customer impact, an urgent court settlement must be
paid within days to avoid penalties or contempt findings, a
critical security incident requiring specialized remediation
consulting to prevent data loss or breach, or a critical
vendor demands immediate payment to avoid suspending
essential services may qualify. Emergency purchases must
still follow approval thresholds and obtain CFO sign-off
within five business days on a retroactive approval form.
You cannot simply declare something an emergency to skip
the normal process and avoid scrutiny. The purchase must be
genuinely urgent and the delay caused by requisitioning
must pose a real cost or risk that can be demonstrated to
the CFO. Emergencies manufactured to skip process steps
are treated as control violations.

Professional services such as audits, legal work, or
specialized consulting are also requisitions, but they
require additional documentation beyond the standard form.
Include the scope of work statement, the service
provider's qualifications or certifications, the contract
term (fixed project, time and materials, retainer, or
hourly), and any insurance or liability requirements. Do
not rely on email exchanges or handshake deals with a
consultant. A scope document and clear pricing prevent
disputes later and make it clear what you expect to receive
for the money. If you are retaining legal counsel for a
complex contract negotiation, specify whether the
engagement is flat-fee or hourly, the expected duration,
and the key deliverables (a negotiated contract, advice
on specific clauses, etc.). Software subscriptions and
cloud services are requisitions as well, though they
benefit from an annual or multi-year commitment to simplify
renewal, reduce per-unit costs, and lock in pricing against
inflation. If you are deploying a third-party software tool
across Northwind to more than 20 users, consult with the
information security team during procurement to ensure the
vendor meets our data handling and compliance standards.
This adds time on the front end but prevents rejections
downstream and avoids adopting software that does not meet
our security or privacy requirements.

Contingent labor or contractor services are requisitions
routed through human resources and finance jointly. A
contractor bringing specialized skills for a defined
project duration is a normal business expense, but the
contractor must meet our hiring and compliance
requirements, including background screening and tax form
completion. The human resources department maintains a
list of approved staffing vendors and contractor
categories. Do not hire a contractor directly and invoice
them retroactively, and do not bypass HR requirements
because you want to move quickly. HR has good reasons for
the vetting process. Intercompany recharges between
departments or between Northwind offices use a different
requisition path, typically an internal transfer order
rather than a purchase order. If you are paying another
Northwind department or office for goods or services,
work with finance to ensure the transaction flows through
the intercompany accounts correctly and does not create
duplicate external purchases. An example is when the IT
department provides infrastructure services to the product
team. The cost is recorded as an internal recharge, not
as an external purchase.

A common mistake is splitting a single purchase into
smaller transactions to avoid a higher approval threshold.
For example, a team lead notices that three network
switches are needed for the data center expansion project.
Each switch costs four thousand dollars. The lead is
tempted to raise three separate purchase requisitions, one
for each switch at four thousand dollars each, to stay
under the five thousand dollar manager approval limit and
avoid routing the approval to the operations director,
which takes longer. This is not permitted. Northwind
strictly prohibits threshold avoidance. The intent and
substance of the purchase matter, not the transaction
structure. Northwind enforces procurement thresholds on
the business decision and business justification, not on
the invoice line. Finance reviews purchase patterns
regularly, and three requisitions for materially identical
items raised on the same date to the same vendor is an
immediate red flag. The requisition will be consolidated
and routed to the appropriate higher approver regardless
of how many separate forms were filed. If you are caught
splitting purchases to circumvent thresholds, the
transaction may be rejected entirely and your manager
notified of the control violation. Repeated offenders may
face restrictions on their approval authority or
additional scrutiny on all future purchases.

Another frequent error is raising a requisition after the
purchase has already been made, goods have been received,
or services have been delivered. This retroactive
requisitioning is a control failure and creates liability
if the vendor was not properly vetted, the purchase was
not authorized, or prices were inflated. All purchases must
be authorized before commitment or payment. If you made a
purchase without a prior requisition, you must file a
retroactive approval form immediately and notify your
manager and the finance team. A vendor who is not
onboarded in our supplier database cannot be paid, so if
you sent an order to an unknown vendor without going
through procurement first, the invoice cannot be processed
and the vendor will not receive payment until onboarding
is complete. This creates a liability for Northwind and
puts the vendor in an awkward position. Purchases that
bypass requisitions without retroactive approval within
five business days are subject to recovery action against
the department or employee responsible, regardless of
whether the goods or services were useful or if the
purchase was well-intentioned. Recovery action can
include a deduction from departmental budget, suspension
of purchasing authority, or escalation to executive
leadership.

A third common error is using a renewal form for a scope
increase. A contract renewal extends an existing agreement
at agreed-upon terms and pricing for another period. A
scope increase adds new deliverables, extends services
into new areas, changes the number of users or licenses,
or materially changes the nature of the work. Scope
increases require new pricing and may trigger a new
approval cycle, even if the vendor is known and previously
approved. If your vendor says they can add a new line of
service to an existing contract and you simply renew at
the old price, the vendor has no obligation to deliver the
new scope, and finance has no record of the new terms.
This creates misunderstanding and conflict later. Always
requisition scope changes separately and attach the
amended statement of work or new quote reflecting the
expanded scope. For example, if you have a retainer with
a consulting firm and want to add a new service line,
that requires a separate amendment and may require
re-approval.

Requisitions that are missing required quotes, lack a
business justification, or name unapproved vendors are
returned to the requester by the finance team. Take time
on the front end to gather quotes, explain why this
purchase is necessary, and confirm your vendor is active
in our supplier database. The approval process will be
faster and smoother. A two-day delay to collect quotes
beats a two-week delay because the requisition was
returned three times for missing information. Finance
staff will send rejection notices explaining what is
missing and provide a deadline for resubmission, typically
five business days.

## Completing the Purchase Requisition (PF-201)

The purchase requisition, known internally as PF-201,
is the standard form for all procurement requests below
the emergency threshold. It captures the what, why, who,
and cost of your purchase and routes it to the
appropriate approver based on amount and cost centre.
Filling out PF-201 correctly the first time reduces
returns and delays and gets your purchase into the hands
of the vendor faster. This section covers each field of
the form in detail and explains the common reasons
requisitions are returned so you can avoid them. PF-201
is available in the finance portal and is also
distributed as a PDF template for offline completion if
needed, though online submission is preferred and
results in faster processing.

**Cost centre and general ledger account coding.** Every
requisition must specify a cost centre and general ledger
account. Your cost centre represents your department or
team and is used to track spending and budget consumption.
The cost centre code also routes the approval workflow to
the correct manager based on the cost centre's manager
assignment in the finance system. If you are unsure of
your cost centre code, ask your manager or check the
cost centre directory in the finance portal. The finance
team maintains an updated list of all cost centres, their
manager assignments, and their current budget status. If
you assign the requisition to the wrong cost centre, it
will be routed to the wrong person for approval or
rejected outright if the cost centre does not exist.
Using the correct cost centre is critical to smooth
processing. The general ledger account tells accounting
which line on the income statement to record the expense
and ensures consistent financial reporting across the
company. Common accounts include Contracted Labor,
Software Licenses, Facilities and Maintenance,
Professional Fees, Office Supplies, Equipment, Hardware
Maintenance, Cloud Services, Travel, and Training.
Accounting uses the GL account to produce monthly
financial statements and budgeting forecasts. If you
select the wrong account, accounting will correct it,
but doing so manually adds time to processing and
creates extra work and potential delays. Consult the
chart of accounts document in the finance portal or
email finance@northwind.example if you are uncertain
which account applies to your purchase. The chart of
accounts lists account numbers, names, and detailed
descriptions of what should be charged to each account.

**Vendor selection and master data validation.** Name
the vendor you intend to purchase from and confirm that
the vendor exists in the Northwind supplier master
database. New vendors require additional setup before
any payment can be issued, which is described in detail
in the Vendor Onboarding section below. If your vendor
is not yet in the system, note that in the form and
allow time for onboarding before the desired delivery
date. Do not assume a new vendor will be ready
immediately. Vendor onboarding takes two to four weeks
on average, and sometimes longer if the vendor is small
or unfamiliar with the process. If you are sourcing from
a new vendor for the first time, start the onboarding
process as soon as you know you will need them, not
after you have already placed an order. Purchasing from
an unapproved vendor or a vendor not yet vetted and
entered into the master database will cause the
requisition to be returned and the purchase to be
blocked. Finance will not issue a purchase order or
payment to an unknown vendor under any circumstances
except in rare emergency situations with CFO approval.
If you are working with a vendor for the first time,
plan ahead and start onboarding as soon as you know you
will need them. For existing vendors, double-check the
spelling of the vendor name in the master database
exactly as it appears in the finance system. A
misspelled name or a slightly different legal entity
name can cause routing issues and prevent invoices from
matching to the original purchase order, creating
reconciliation headaches.

**Quote attachment and competitive pricing
requirements.** If the purchase amount is ten thousand
dollars or more, attach at least two written quotes
from different vendors. At fifty thousand dollars or
more, attach three quotes. This is a hard rule with no
exceptions. Quotes must be current and representative
of market conditions as set by the Proposal and Quote
Process. Quotes must cover the same scope of work or
goods so that prices can be meaningfully compared. A quote from one vendor for a basic
configuration and a quote from another for an upgraded
version is not a valid comparison and will be rejected.
For example, if you are purchasing networking equipment,
a quote for managed switches from Vendor A and a quote
for unmanaged switches from Vendor B cannot be compared
directly because the scope is different. Include quotes
even if one is significantly higher or lower than the
others, and explain the material difference in the
business justification field. For example, if Vendor A
quotes a solution without implementation support and
Vendor B includes three days of implementation, the
price difference is expected and should be explained so
the approver understands why one is more expensive.
Attach quotes as PDF or image files directly to the
PF-201 form in the finance portal. Do not paste quote
text into the text box or attach Word documents, as this
makes comparison harder and can cause processing delays.
For purchases under ten thousand dollars, quotes are
not required by policy, but it is good practice to
compare at least two vendors informally to ensure you
are getting reasonable value for money.

**Business justification and scope of work statement.**
Describe what you are purchasing, why it is needed, and
how it supports Northwind's operations or strategy.
Vague descriptions are a leading cause of requisition
rejection. Finance reviewers and approvers need clear
context to make a decision. For example, "New laptop for
Sarah Chen, Level 3 Product Designer, to replace
existing device with degraded performance that impairs
design work and slows hiring integration" is far clearer
and more persuasive than simply "Equipment." A hiring
manager approving the purchase can see the business
reason, the impact on the employee, and the justification
for the expense. For software or contracted services,
include the business justification, contract term
(monthly, annual, three-year), and any special terms
such as a discount tied to annual payment or a volume
commitment. Include the implementation or deployment
plan if applicable. For example, "Procurement of cloud
analytics platform on a 12-month commitment at 20
percent discount for annual upfront payment, with
implementation and training included. Deployment planned
for Q4 to support company data strategy initiative.
Expected to reduce reporting time by 40 percent and
enable self-service analytics for department heads."
That level of detail helps approvers evaluate the
business case. If you are renewing a purchase, reference
the original requisition or purchase order number so
accounting can track the historical spend and understand
this is a renewal rather than a new vendor or service.
The finance team uses this information to monitor vendor
performance and budget trends.

**Delivery address, billing address, and service
location.** Specify where the goods should be delivered
or services performed with precise location details. For
example, if the purchase is equipment, state the office
location and department. If the purchase is software or
cloud services, note if there are any location or
jurisdictional requirements, such as data residency in a
specific country for compliance reasons. If billing
should go to a different address than delivery, specify
both addresses clearly. Some vendors use a billing
address on file for invoicing and a delivery address for
each order. Getting this right on the requisition
prevents delays when the order is placed and prevents
goods from being shipped to the wrong location or
invoiced to the wrong department. Include a contact
name and phone number for the delivery address if the
vendor might need to follow up on delivery logistics.

**Currency and foreign exchange treatment.** If you are
purchasing from a vendor outside the United States,
specify the currency of the quote and the expected
foreign exchange rate used for budgeting. Northwind's
reporting currency is US dollars. Finance will convert
invoices from foreign vendors at the prevailing exchange
rate on the invoice date unless a fixed forward rate was
negotiated with the vendor. If a significant FX swing
is a risk (for example, you are purchasing from a
vendor in British pounds and the pound is volatile),
mention that in the requisition and consider negotiating
a fixed rate with the vendor before placing the order.
A fixed forward rate locks in the USD equivalent and
removes FX uncertainty. Do not assume the vendor will
absorb currency changes or accept payment in US dollars.
Get pricing in USD if possible to avoid FX surprises,
but be prepared to pay in the vendor's local currency
if that is required.

**Multi-year commitments and renewal terms.** If the
purchase involves a multi-year contract or automatic
renewal, state the term, the renewal terms, and any
price increases that are built in. For example, a
software license may have a three-year term with
automatic annual renewal and a two percent price
increase each year, which means the Year 2 cost is
2 percent higher than Year 1, and Year 3 is 2 percent
higher than Year 2. Document this in the requisition so
approvers and finance can plan for the full cost of the
commitment and include it in budget forecasts. If the
vendor offers a discount for upfront payment of the full
term, include that in the business justification. A
vendor may quote a 15 percent discount for upfront
payment of three years versus annual renewals. That
difference is worth noting so the approver can see the
total cost impact and understand the economics of the
choice.

**Approval routing, thresholds, and timelines.** Once
submitted, PF-201 routes automatically to the
appropriate approver based on the amount and the cost
centre's manager assignment. For amounts under five
thousand dollars, approval is at the manager level and
typically completes within two business days if the
requisition has all required information. For five
thousand dollars to fifty thousand dollars, approval is
by the Operations Director and usually takes three to
five business days. For amounts above fifty thousand
dollars, the Chief Financial Officer must approve and
the review may take longer if legal, security, or
compliance questions arise. You can track the status of
your requisition in the finance portal and see who is
currently reviewing it and how long they have had it.
If you do not hear back within the expected timeframe,
follow up with the approver. Delays sometimes occur if
the approver is traveling or out of the office, but
your follow-up helps prioritize the request.

**The six most common reasons for PF-201 rejection.**
The most frequent reason a requisition is returned is
missing or invalid quotes. If your purchase is over ten
thousand dollars and you have not provided two dated
quotes from different vendors, or if the quotes cover
different scopes, finance will send the form back with a
request to resubmit with compliant quotes. Do not say
you will attach quotes later. Get quotes before
submitting. Suppliers typically provide quotes via email
within one to two business days if you ask, so plan
accordingly.

The second common reason is an incomplete or missing
business justification. Approvers need to understand
what is being purchased and why. A line that simply
says "Supplies" or "Service" is not enough information
to approve. Provide context so an approver can make an
informed decision without asking follow-up questions.
Vague justifications cause rejection.

The third reason is an invalid or unapproved vendor.
If the vendor is not in the master database and you
have not flagged them as new and requiring onboarding,
the requisition will be returned. Finance will not
proceed with a purchase from an unknown vendor. The
fourth reason is an incorrect or missing cost centre
code. If the cost centre does not exist or does not
match your department, the requisition will be routed
to the wrong person or rejected outright. Verify the
cost centre code before submitting.

The fifth reason is an incorrect or missing general
ledger account. If you select a chart of accounts code
that does not exist or does not match the nature of the
expense, accounting will reject it. The sixth reason is
missing or unclear delivery and billing address
information, especially for international shipments or
multi-location services. Ambiguous addresses cause the
vendor to ask for clarification, which delays the order.

Avoid these common pitfalls by reviewing the PF-201
checklist before hitting submit: cost centre confirmed
with your manager, vendor in master database or flagged
as new for onboarding, quotes attached and dated within
30 days if required, business justification written in
clear language and specific to your need, delivery
address specified with contact details, currency noted
if foreign purchase, and any multi-year terms
documented clearly. Take five minutes on the front end
to prevent a return that delays your purchase by days
or weeks.

## Vendor Onboarding and the Supplier Packet

Northwind does not issue payment to any vendor until
the vendor has been formally registered in the supplier
master database and security checks are complete. Vendor
onboarding is a compliance and risk management
requirement, and it must be finished before the first
invoice is paid. If you are working with a new vendor,
allow time for onboarding before the purchase is needed.
Attempting to rush or skip onboarding to meet a deadline
is not permitted. This section explains the onboarding
process, the documents required, the timeline involved,
the security review process, and the critical importance
of verifying banking information changes through a
formal process.

**Vendor master setup and the supplier packet (VP-12).**
When a new vendor is identified, they receive the
Northwind Supplier Packet, referred to as VP-12, which
is a comprehensive set of forms and requirements the
vendor must complete and return. The supplier packet is
sent by email to the vendor's finance or administrative
contact and includes detailed instructions on what is
required and why. The packet includes a vendor
information sheet with company name, address, and
contact details, banking details for payment routing
(wire instructions, ACH account and routing numbers, or
IBAN for international vendors), a W-9 tax form or
equivalent if the vendor is outside the US, proof of
business license or articles of incorporation, and
certificates of insurance if the vendor provides
services on-site or handles company data. The vendor
also completes a security questionnaire to confirm they
maintain reasonable safeguards for data and comply with
relevant regulations. Northwind does not accept payment
to a vendor until all elements of the VP-12 are
received, reviewed, and approved by accounting and the
information security team. The vendor is then added to
the master database and invoices can be paid. The entire
process is tracked in a vendor management system to
ensure nothing is overlooked.

The VP-12 packet takes time to complete and return,
especially if the vendor is small or unfamiliar with the
process. Budget two to four weeks for a new vendor to
complete onboarding. Large vendors or those with
existing relationships with other Northwind offices may
complete it faster because they are familiar with the
process and may already have some required documentation
on file. The Accounts Payable team sends a reminder
email after one week if the packet has not been
returned. The IT Security team reviews the security
questionnaire to ensure the vendor's practices align
with Northwind standards and legal requirements. The IT
Security team checks for encryption of data at rest and
in transit, a written data protection policy, incident
response procedures, compliance with relevant standards
such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001, and background information
on the vendor's security history. Any red flags in the
security review, such as no written data protection
policy, lack of encryption, missing insurance, or a
history of security incidents, can delay onboarding or
result in additional contractual terms. Northwind may
require the vendor to purchase cyber liability insurance,
implement specific technical controls, complete a more
detailed security audit, or agree to enhanced
contractual data protection clauses before approval.
These requirements are documented and become part of
the vendor agreement. Once onboarding is complete, the
vendor remains in good standing until something changes,
such as a banking information update, a change of
ownership, a merger or acquisition, or a security
incident reported by the vendor or discovered by
Northwind.

For vendors handling sensitive company data or providing
critical services, security requirements are more
stringent. A vendor that will process employee personal
data, access customer information, or maintain company
intellectual property must demonstrate strong data
protection practices. Northwind may require the vendor
to complete a detailed security assessment, provide a
SOC 2 audit report, or sign a data processing agreement
specifying how they will protect information. The
information security team will review these items
carefully and may engage external security consultants
to verify the vendor's claims. This process takes
additional time but is necessary to ensure company data
is protected. Vendors have an obligation to notify
Northwind of any security incidents they experience,
whether or not company data is affected. If a vendor
experiences a breach and does not report it, Northwind
may terminate the vendor relationship and pursue
recovery for damages.

**Banking details and the vendor master change form
(PF-204).** If an existing vendor notifies you that
their banking details have changed, do not process the
change immediately through the normal vendor update
process. Banking details are sensitive financial
information and are a common vector for payment fraud
and payment diversion attacks. Northwind requires
verification of banking changes before updating the
supplier database to prevent fraud. To update a
vendor's banking information, the vendor must complete
a Vendor Master Data Change form, called PF-204. The
PF-204 requires the vendor to provide new banking
details including the bank name, account number and
routing number (for US vendors), or IBAN and BIC (for
international vendors), and is submitted to the
Northwind accounts payable team via the finance portal
or by secure email. Upon receipt of the PF-204,
accounts payable calls the vendor back at a telephone
number that is already known to Northwind, such as the
number listed on a previous invoice, the vendor's
website, or a past contract. An authorized person at
the vendor (usually someone in finance or accounts
receivable) confirms the banking change verbally and
often provides verbal authorization to update the
master record. This callback verification step confirms
the change request is legitimate and comes from the
vendor, not from a third party attempting to intercept
payments for their own benefit.

This two-person verification process (the PF-204 form
plus the callback to a known number) is in place because
invoicing fraud and payment diversion is a significant
financial risk to the company. An attacker who learns
that Company A usually pays Vendor B for regular
services on a monthly basis could contact Company A
claiming to be Vendor B and request a banking change to
a new account. If the banking change is processed
without verification, all subsequent invoices are
diverted to the attacker's account instead of the
legitimate vendor. The legitimate vendor never receives
payment and may stop providing services or take legal
action against Northwind for non-payment. Northwind has
seen this attack before and has lost money to it. A
vendor called expecting a wire transfer for a service
that was delivered, but the wire went to a fake account
provided by an attacker, and by the time the fraud was
discovered, the attacker had moved the funds and they
were unrecoverable. Callback verification prevents this
by confirming that a real person at the vendor
authorized the change before the database is updated.
Do not update a vendor's banking information based on
email alone, even if the email appears to come from a
known contact at the vendor, was signed with a company
email signature, or included company letterhead scanned
as an attachment. Email is easily spoofed and no amount
of signatures makes it secure. A convincing phishing
email impersonating a vendor's CFO or Finance Director
has compromised many companies and diverted millions in
payments. Follow the PF-204 process and the callback
verification step every single time without exception,
regardless of how urgent the vendor claims the change
is or how well you think you know the vendor.

**Worked example of a banking change fraud attempt.**
A vendor called Global Supply Chain Partners sends an
email to the Northwind accounts payable inbox claiming
that they have switched banks and want to update their
wire instructions. The email appears to come from
John.Smith@GlobalSupply.example and is signed "John
Smith, Finance Manager, Global Supply Chain Partners."
The email looks plausible, includes a company signature
with a logo, and even references a recent purchase
order number. Someone in the accounts payable team is
tempted to update the banking information immediately
based on the email to keep things moving. However,
because this is a vendor banking change, the accounts
payable team member insists that Global Supply Chain
Partners complete the PF-204 form instead and follows
the protocol. The vendor (attacker) delays and makes
excuses about why they cannot access the finance
portal. The Northwind team member then calls the phone
number on file for Global Supply Chain Partners from a
previous invoice and speaks to the actual John Smith,
who works in finance at Global Supply Chain Partners.
The actual John Smith has no idea what this banking
change is about and says they have not requested any
banking change. The actual vendor has not switched
banks and does not plan to. The email was a phishing
attempt by someone trying to reroute Northwind's
payment to their own account. The fraud is caught
before payment is sent to the wrong account, and
Northwind forwards the phishing email to the
information security team and law enforcement. Without
the PF-204 requirement and callback verification,
Northwind would have sent hundreds of thousands of
dollars to an attacker's account over the following
months.

**Vendor performance and contract reviews.** After a
vendor is onboarded, the relationship is monitored
through the contract period to ensure the vendor
continues to meet Northwind standards. Northwind conducts
periodic reviews of vendor performance, security posture,
and compliance. If a vendor's performance declines or
quality issues emerge, accounting and the contract owner
work with the vendor to address the issues. Serious
performance failures or security incidents may result in
termination of the vendor relationship. Vendors are also
re-vetted before contract renewal if significant time
has passed or if security requirements have changed. The
procurement process does not end with the first invoice;
it is an ongoing partnership that requires attention.

**Insurance and liability requirements.** Depending on
the nature of the work, a vendor may be required to
carry liability insurance or other coverage. Vendors
that provide services on-site, handle equipment, or
interact with employees must carry general liability
insurance. Vendors that process company data must carry
cyber liability insurance. Vendors that manage company
funds must carry fidelity insurance. The VP-12 packet
asks vendors to provide certificates of insurance
showing current coverage. Finance reviews these
certificates to ensure the coverage amounts are
sufficient and the coverage is in force. If a vendor
cannot provide proof of required insurance, Northwind
will not proceed with the relationship until insurance
is in place. This protects Northwind from liability if
a vendor causes damage or harm.

## Exceptions, Emergencies and Retroactive Approval

Northwind's standard procurement process is thorough but
takes time to execute properly. Emergency situations
sometimes arise where waiting for a requisition and full
approval cycle is not feasible. In these rare cases,
procurement can be bypassed with proper authorization,
but the purchase must be regularized afterward through a
retroactive approval process. This section explains when
emergency procurement applies, how to obtain retroactive
approval, how vendor vetting works in emergencies, and
how Northwind monitors and audits emergency purchases
to prevent abuse of the exception.

**When emergency procurement applies.** An emergency
purchase is one made without a prior requisition due to
an urgent, unforeseen business need. The need must be
genuine and the delay caused by the normal process must
pose a real business cost, operational risk, or
compliance risk. Examples include an unplanned equipment
failure that stops production and requires an immediate
replacement part to resume operations and prevent
customer impact, an urgent court settlement that must be
paid within days to avoid penalties or contempt
findings, a critical security incident requiring
specialized consulting to contain and remediate and
prevent data loss or breach, or a critical vendor
demanding immediate payment to avoid stopping essential
services. A purchase made simply because the requester
forgot to plan ahead or chose not to follow the process
is not an emergency. Procuring from a vendor that has
not completed onboarding because you were in a hurry to
place an order is also not an emergency. You should have
ordered from a vendor already in the system if you had
planned properly. True emergencies are uncommon and
should remain so. Most urgent business needs can be
handled through expedited requisitioning if you ask the
manager and the approver to prioritize the request.
Expedited approval on a normal requisition is faster
and simpler than emergency bypass with retroactive
approval.

**Retroactive approval and the PF-330 form.** Any
emergency purchase made without a prior requisition
must be formalized within five business days on a
retroactive approval form, called PF-330. The five
business day deadline is absolute. Emergency purchases
that are not approved retroactively within five business
days are treated as control violations and may trigger
recovery action. The PF-330 documents what was
purchased, the vendor, the amount, the date the
purchase was made, the date payment was made or is due,
the business reason for the emergency, and the person
who authorized the purchase. The form is routed to the
Chief Financial Officer, Marcus Webb, for signature and
approval regardless of the purchase amount. The CFO
will not approve a retroactive purchase if the stated
reason does not justify the bypass of normal controls or
if the vendor is not in good standing or is not yet
onboarded. The CFO will also question or deny approval
if the "emergency" reason sounds pretextual (such as "we
forgot to requisition" or "we wanted it faster"). Once
signed by the CFO, the purchase is authorized
retroactively and the transaction is recorded in the
accounting system with a flag indicating it was approved
after the fact for audit and financial reporting
purposes. Failure to obtain retroactive approval within
five business days is a control violation and can result
in recovery of the purchase amount from the department
or employee responsible. If you make an emergency
purchase and forget to file the PF-330, you are putting
your department at financial risk and may face budget
reductions or salary deductions to recover the
unauthorized amount.

**Vendor vetting requirements in emergencies.** If an
emergency purchase is made from a vendor not yet in the
supplier master database, onboarding must still be
completed before payment can be issued. Do not assume
you can skip vendor onboarding because the purchase was
urgent. The urgency applies to when you can make the
purchase and place an order, not to whether the vendor
must be vetted before payment. Northwind has a
zero-tolerance policy on paying unapproved vendors
under any circumstances. If payment is due to the
vendor before onboarding is complete, inform your
manager and the CFO immediately. In rare cases, interim
payment arrangements can be made, such as paying into
an escrow account pending final vendor approval, but
this requires CFO authorization and is documented
separately on the PF-330 with full details of the
escrow arrangement. Do not simply wire money to an
unknown vendor and hope onboarding happens later.
Finance will not release payment for an unapproved
vendor, and if the money was sent without
authorization, recovery may be difficult or impossible.
The only exception to vendor vetting is if a vendor is
already in the system from a previous engagement. In
that case, an emergency purchase can proceed without
additional onboarding, but invoices must still be
processed through normal accounts payable channels.

**Recovery and audit of emergency purchases.**
Departments and approvers are accountable for emergency
purchase activity and must use emergencies only when
justified. Finance conducts quarterly audits of all
emergency purchases made in the previous three months
to ensure they truly justified the bypass of normal
controls and that retroactive approvals were obtained
on time. The audit includes spot-checks of the stated
business reasons against actual business events (did a
server failure actually occur on that date, or is the
date fabricated), review of whether the purchase could
have been planned and requisitioned normally, and
confirmation that vendors were approved or onboarded
before payment was issued. Finance may request
additional documentation such as work orders, incident
reports, or emails showing the emergency nature of the
request. Repeated or questionable emergency purchases
result in escalation to the CFO and the relevant
executive sponsor. Cost centres with high emergency
purchase rates may see their manager approval limits
reduced or require additional review steps until the
pattern improves. For example, if a department has made
more than three emergency purchases in a quarter
without clear justification, the CFO may require that
all future purchases over ten thousand dollars for that
department be pre-approved by the CFO regardless of
amount, or require the department head to sign a
commitment to plan better and build contingency into
timelines. Use emergency procurement as the exception
it is meant to be, not as an alternative to planning
ahead. Finance tracks all emergency purchases in a
master audit log and reports quarterly to the audit
committee and the CFO on trends and patterns. If a
specific cost centre or manager shows a pattern of
questionable emergencies, the CFO will meet with that
manager to review the issue and set expectations for
improvement.

**Sole source and single-vendor justification.**
Occasionally, only one vendor can meet a genuine
business need. If a company uses proprietary software
that only one vendor can support, or if only one vendor
carries specialized equipment, a sole-source purchase
may be justified. Sole-source purchases still require
requisitions and approvals, and they still follow
thresholds. Sole-source justification is not the same
as emergency procurement. You must explain in the
requisition why only one vendor can meet the need and
why competitive quotes cannot be obtained. Vague
sole-source claims are rejected. Finance audits
sole-source justifications to ensure they are not being
used to bypass the competitive process. If sole-source
purchases appear frequently from the same cost centre,
an audit may determine that threshold avoidance or
improper vendor relationships are at play.

**Incident-driven purchases and business continuity.**
A business interruption can justify emergency
procurement if operations have stopped and an immediate
purchase is required to restore service. For example, if
a critical data center link fails and a replacement card
is needed immediately to restore connectivity, an
emergency purchase from a vendor is justified.
Incident-driven purchases must be documented with the
incident ticket or incident report number on the
PF-330. Finance can verify the incident occurred and
the timing. If an incident report does not exist for the
stated emergency date, the PF-330 will be rejected
unless the department can provide strong additional
evidence. Business continuity and disaster recovery
planning should reduce the frequency of incident-driven
purchases by ensuring backup vendors and spare parts
are available. Departments are responsible for
maintaining appropriate spare inventory and vendor
relationships to prevent operational failures.

**Control compliance and management responsibility.**
Managers whose departments use emergency procurement
frequently are accountable for improving planning. The
CFO will discuss emergency purchase patterns with
relevant managers and may impose additional controls,
such as requiring advanced vendor approval or budget
allocation for contingency items. A manager who
repeatedly bypasses the normal process may be removed
from approval authority. The objective is not to punish
emergency use in genuine cases, but to ensure
procurement controls are respected and planning
improves. Finance and the CFO take procurement
seriously because it is one of the highest-value
operational processes and a vector for fraud,
unauthorized spending, and vendor manipulation.
Following the process is not bureaucracy; it is risk
management.

**Questions and escalation.** If you are uncertain
whether a purchase qualifies as an emergency or how to
complete a PF-330, contact finance@northwind.example
before making the purchase. Finance can advise on
whether emergency procurement is appropriate or if
expedited requisitioning will meet your timeline.
Questions early prevent rejections and violations later.
