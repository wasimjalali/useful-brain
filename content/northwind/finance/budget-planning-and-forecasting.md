# Budget Planning and Forecasting

## Purpose and Ownership

This document describes how Northwind Systems plans and forecasts its
budget. It is owned by finance, and its readers are the finance team and
the executive leadership. The budget cycle runs from the calendar year
fiscal year (January 1 to December 31). The CFO, Marcus Webb, owns the
process, and the CEO, Dana Okafor, approves the final budget at the
December board meeting, which is covered by the Board Reporting Calendar.
Department heads are responsible for submitting their department budgets
on time; the process is the same every year, and the deadlines in this
document are fixed.

## Budget Cycle Timeline

The annual cycle has five milestones. June 1: finance publishes the
budget instructions and templates. July 15: department heads submit
their initial budget requests. August 15: finance returns first-pass
feedback and departments revise. September 30: departments submit final
budgets. November 1: the executive team reviews the consolidated budget
with the CFO, and the CEO approves it. December board meeting (the
second Tuesday of December): the board ratifies the budget. The
November 1 review is the last point at which department totals can
change; after that, changes require the CFO's written approval. The
merit increase pool for the following year is fixed during the
September 30 submission at 3% of payroll by default.

## Department Budget Structure

Each department budget has four envelopes: payroll, travel and
expenses, tools and software, and capital. Payroll includes salaries,
bonus accruals, and contractor spend, and is validated against the
Salary Bands document by HR before submission. Travel and expenses are
planned at 80% of the prior year's actual spend by default, and any
department requesting more must attach a written justification. Tools
and software budgets are reviewed by finance for overlap with the
corporate tool list maintained by IT. Capital (equipment, office
furnishings) is approved separately by operations under the Vendor and
Procurement Policy's thresholds, but the budget line is set here.

## Forecasting During the Year

Forecasting is a quarterly activity. Finance publishes a rolling
forecast on the 15th of February, May, August, and November, comparing
actuals to the budget and projecting the year-end position. Department
heads review the forecast with finance within five business days of
publication. A department forecast that exceeds its budget by more than
5% triggers a formal review with the CFO. Revenue forecasting is owned
by finance with input from sales: the sales forecast feeds the
commission accrual (see the Sales Commission Plan) and the cash
projection. The forecast is not a commitment; only the approved budget
controls spending, and the Vendor and Procurement Policy's approval
thresholds apply to actual purchases.

## Spending Rules

Department budgets are annual and do not roll over; unused budget at
December 31 is returned to the company, except for the training budget
rules in the Training and Development Budget. Mid-year reallocation
between envelopes within a department is allowed up to $50,000 per
reallocation with the CFO's approval, and reallocation between
departments is not allowed at all. Hiring beyond the budgeted headcount
requires the CEO's approval and is reviewed against the approved
headcount plan; the Recruiting and Interview Policy's offer thresholds
still apply. Emergency spending above the department budget requires
the CFO's approval and is reported to the board at the next board
meeting.

## Budget vs Actuals Reporting

Finance publishes a monthly budget-vs-actuals report to department heads
by the 10th of the following month. The report shows actuals, budget,
variance, and a variance explanation column that departments must
complete for variances above 10%. The April and October SOC 2 audits
include a sample of the monthly reports, and the annual audit of
financial statements happens in January, using the December 31
year-end. The board receives a summary of the year-end position at the
February board meeting, per the Board Reporting Calendar. Variance
reports are retained for seven years under the Data Retention Policy.

## Key Planning Assumptions

The standard planning assumptions are published with the June 1
instructions and rarely change: revenue growth of 15% year over year,
merit pool of 3% of payroll, travel budget at 80% of prior year actuals,
and a 2% of payroll training allocation. Headcount planning assumes one
new hire for every $350,000 of incremental annual recurring revenue
booked, which ties hiring to the sales forecast. Exchange rates for the
budget use the November finance rate and are fixed for the budget year.
The CFO can revise planning assumptions mid-year only for material
events, and any revision is communicated to department heads within five
business days.
