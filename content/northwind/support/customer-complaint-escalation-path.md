# Customer Complaint Escalation Path

## Overview

This policy defines how Northwind Systems handles customer complaints
that are not resolved by normal support work. It is separate from the
technical Incident Escalation Matrix, which covers system incidents
(SEV-1 through SEV-4) and is owned by engineering. A complaint is a
customer statement that they are unhappy with the service, the billing,
or our conduct, and that they expect something beyond a ticket
resolution. Complaints get their own codes (ESC-####) and their own
escalation levels, ESC-1 through ESC-4. The path is owned by the VP of
Customer Support, Nadia Petrov, and this document is shared with sales
because account managers often hear complaints first.

## ESC-1: Complaint Logged

A complaint is logged as ESC-1 when the support agent identifies that
the customer is raising a complaint rather than a routine issue, or
when a customer asks to "speak to someone about how this was handled."
The agent logs the complaint in the support system with an ESC-####
code, attaches the ticket history, and responds to the customer within
four business hours with an acknowledgment and a named owner. The
acknowledgment does not promise a resolution; it confirms the complaint
is being reviewed. The response time target for ESC-1 is measured from
the moment the complaint is logged, not from the original ticket. If
the agent is unsure whether something is a complaint, they log it as
one; over-logging is preferred to under-logging.

## ESC-2: Support Manager Review

If the complaint is not resolved within two business days of the ESC-1
acknowledgment, it escalates to ESC-2 and the support manager takes
over. The support manager reviews the full history, calls the customer
within one business day of taking over, and can resolve directly or
assign a senior agent. ESC-2 resolution includes a written summary of
what happened and what will change. The support manager can offer
goodwill compensation up to $500 without further approval. If the
customer is still not satisfied after the ESC-2 resolution attempt, or
if the complaint involves billing amounts over $500, the complaint
moves to ESC-3. Complaints that mention legal action, regulators, or
the media move straight to ESC-3 regardless of the timeline.

## ESC-3: VP Support

ESC-3 is triggered when: the complaint is unresolved after five
business days from ESC-1, the customer mentions legal action or a
regulator, the goodwill amount needed exceeds $500, or the account's
annual value is above $100,000. The VP of Customer Support takes
ownership, and for complaints that involve legal or regulatory
mention, the General Counsel is copied from this point. The VP responds
to the customer within two business days. Goodwill authority at ESC-3
is up to $2,500 for the VP; anything above $2,500 requires finance and
legal approval together. The VP can also approve service credits,
extended support terms, or an account review. ESC-3 complaints are
reviewed in the weekly support leadership meeting.

## ESC-4: Executive Complaints

ESC-4 is reserved for complaints from a customer's executive team
(CEO, CFO, or board member) or complaints that the customer's executive
has personally escalated. At ESC-4, the CEO's office is notified within
four business hours, and the CEO's office responds to the customer
within 24 hours. The VP of Support coordinates the response and the
remediation plan, and the CEO's office owns the communication. ESC-4
complaints are reported to the board at the next board meeting per the
Board Reporting Calendar. Settlement or goodwill at ESC-4 follows the
same finance and legal approval rules as ESC-3 above $2,500; there is
no executive override of the approval chain, but the CEO's office can
expedite the review.

## Goodwill and Compensation Rules

Goodwill compensation is tracked against the complaint code and is not
a refund: the Refund Policy governs refunds, and goodwill is
compensation for inconvenience. Limits: support managers can offer up
to $500, the VP of Support up to $2,500, and amounts above $2,500 need
joint approval from finance and legal. Goodwill can be a service
credit, a fee waiver, or a payment, and anything paid as money follows
the Refund Policy's payment processing (5 to 10 business days to the
original payment method). Goodwill offers are never framed as
admissions of liability, and the Dispute Resolution and Arbitration
Policy applies if a customer rejects the resolution and pursues a
claim.

## Complaint Records

Complaints are logged with their ESC-#### codes, and the log records:
the trigger, the level reached, the resolution, and the goodwill amount.
The log is reviewed monthly by the VP of Support for patterns, and
complaints that reveal a product defect are routed to engineering under
the Incident Escalation Matrix. Complaint records are customer
relationship records and are kept for seven years from the complaint
date under the Data Retention Policy. The quarterly complaint report
feeds the Customer Satisfaction metrics in the Agent Performance
Scorecard and the April and October SOC 2 audit evidence.

## Relationship to Other Policies

The Support SLA Policy governs routine ticket response and resolution
times (P0 through P3); a complaint that is also a P0 or P1 ticket runs
both tracks in parallel, and the SLA times still apply. The Refund
Policy governs refund requests and windows (14 calendar days for annual
plans); complaints do not extend refund windows, but refund requests
made during a complaint are logged against the complaint code. The
Invoicing and Payment Terms Policy governs billing disputes, which
move to this path after 30 days unresolved. Complaints about data
handling are routed to the Customer Data Access Requests process and
the Privacy Policy.
